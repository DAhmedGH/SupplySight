# Historical inventory intelligence

## Purpose and scope

The inventory intelligence layer evaluates a planning decision as of **2024-12-31** against the persisted January–March 2025 demand forecast. It is a historical validation exercise, not a set of current 2026 inventory recommendations. The outputs are decision support: they neither create purchase orders nor guarantee delivery or transfer feasibility.

The layer reads eligible `MARTS.INVENTORY_DETAIL`, `MARTS.SUPPLIER_DETAIL`, `MARTS.SALES_ORDER_LINE`, and `MARTS.SHIPMENT_DETAIL` rows, the CORE date and warehouse/product dimensions needed to resolve business identifiers, and `FORECASTING.CURRENT_FORECASTS` with `FORECASTING.RUN_METADATA`. It does not read RAW or STAGING. `MARTS.INVENTORY_MONTHLY` remains a descriptive monthly balance mart; the exact cutoff position comes from `INVENTORY_DETAIL`, not an average or a nearest snapshot. All models are pinned to `SUPPLY_CHAIN_DEV` and `SUPPLY_CHAIN_DEV_WH`. No new schema is required.

## Historical as-of contract

Planning requires one successful forecast run observed through 2024-12-31, with three monthly forecasts for January, February, and March 2025 at each product business ID and warehouse. An incompatible replacement run or missing horizon fails validation; it is never combined silently with the historical snapshot. The planning population includes both forecast pairs and eligible cutoff inventory pairs, so an inventory pair with no forecast remains visible as `MISSING_FORECAST_SERIES`. The cutoff snapshot must be dated exactly 2024-12-31. A missing snapshot remains missing and makes risk unassessable, rather than becoming zero stock.

Purchase orders are period-end records without status-event history. The 2024-12-31 source state supports this specific cutoff, but cannot reconstruct arbitrary earlier as-of dates. Only lines ordered by the cutoff enter planning. Lines with an explicitly post-cutoff receipt date are excluded from the selected supplier/lead-time choice. Future-due `in_transit` lines with no receipt date, zero received quantity, and positive remaining quantity are scheduled for their expected delivery date. Overdue `in_transit` and `open` lines remain visible as uncertain inbound and do not enter projected inventory. Snapshot `in_transit_units` overlaps the PO-derived inbound and is never added to it. Post-cutoff realized receipts, later supplier performance, future inventory snapshots, and January–March 2025 realized sales are excluded.

For each product and warehouse, the latest eligible PO ordered by the cutoff supplies the selected supplier and planned lead time. Ranking uses descending order date and a deterministic PO line ID tie-break. Lead time is the positive difference between expected delivery and order dates. The current supplier dimension's lead-time attribute is descriptive context only; its historical state is not verified. Pairs without a PO relationship keep their risk and transfer context but have no reorder quantity or order timing.

## Planning calculations

Each forecast month is prorated uniformly over its calendar days. Daily point demand is monthly point demand divided by days in the month; daily upper demand uses the empirical upper bound in the same way. The daily horizon is 2025-01-01 through 2025-03-31. Calculations preserve fractional precision; only final recommended physical unit quantities are rounded. Missing months or bounds leave dependent planning outputs unavailable. Demand is not extrapolated beyond the horizon.

Daily projected point and stress inventory equal cutoff available units plus cumulative scheduled PO inbound minus cumulative point or upper demand. The first date below zero identifies the corresponding first shortage date. Expected and stressed shortage units are the maximum positive deficits anywhere on their respective paths: extra inventory needed to keep the modeled path nonnegative, not measured lost sales. The upper-demand lead-time excess over point lead-time demand, floored at zero, is the **empirical stress buffer**. The bounds are empirical validation-error bands, not statistical confidence intervals or service-level safety stock. A missing upper bound produces a null buffer, never zero. The product reorder point is descriptive and is not added to the buffer.

A standard PO placed at the cutoff is assumed to arrive on cutoff plus the selected planned lead-time days, available for that day's demand. Suggested reorder units are the ceiling of the maximum nonnegative upper-path deficit from that arrival date through the forecast horizon, after scheduled inbound is included. A positive value means order now. If a shortage occurs before that arrival, `immediate_mitigation_flag` indicates that the standard reorder cannot resolve it. Missing snapshot, supplier relationship, valid lead time, complete horizon, or upper bounds suppresses the reorder quantity with an explicit reason. The model does not assume MOQ, case packs, capacity, order multiples, or an optimized order date.

Stockout risk is a deterministic rule, not a probability. `UNASSESSABLE` means a required snapshot, forecast, or bound is missing. Otherwise zero cutoff available stock is `CRITICAL`; a positive expected shortage is `HIGH`; stress-only shortage is `MEDIUM`; and no modeled shortage is `LOW`. Shortage units and dates accompany the tier.

Historical realized unit value is pre-cutoff delivered recognized net revenue divided by pre-cutoff delivered units from `MARTS.SALES_ORDER_LINE`, grouped by product business ID and warehouse. An eligible delivered shipment must provide dated evidence that the full order-line quantity was delivered before the cutoff; current order status alone is insufficient to establish historical recognition. Returns and refunds remain separate under the sales mart contract. Potential revenue exposure at historical realized unit value equals expected shortage units times that value. When no delivered-unit denominator exists, both the unit value and exposure are null. Exposure is neither guaranteed lost revenue nor a probability-adjusted loss.

Transfer rows are conditional inventory-balancing candidates. A destination can request at most the whole-unit floor of its expected shortage. Source surplus uses only physically available cutoff units after protecting the source against its own full three-month upper-demand path with scheduled inbound accounted for; fractional surplus is rounded down. Future PO units are not offered as transferable stock. Destinations rank by `CRITICAL`, then `HIGH`, then descending expected shortage and warehouse identifier. Sources rank by descending transferable surplus and warehouse identifier. Cumulative need and surplus intervals allocate only their positive overlap, so each source and destination is capped and each physical unit is allocated at most once. Transfer units do not reduce the independent reorder recommendation. Transit time, logistics cost, routing, and capacity feasibility are unknown.

## Published grains and lineage

`int_inventory_planning_supply` is a view at forecast run × PO line. `int_inventory_planning_position` is a view at run × product ID × warehouse. `int_inventory_planning_daily` is a view at run × product ID × warehouse × forecast day. These route to `INTERMEDIATE`. `mart_inventory_planning_monthly` is a table at run × cutoff × product ID × warehouse × forecast month. `mart_inventory_recommendations` is a table at run × cutoff × product ID × warehouse. `mart_inventory_transfers` is a table at run × product ID × source warehouse × destination warehouse, with positive allocations only. These route to `MARTS`.

The outputs retain forecast run and model lineage, selected snapshot and PO keys, supplier identifiers, quality state/issues, source batch fields, and product-history fallback where available. CORE and mart eligibility has already excluded quarantined fact rows; warning rows remain visible. Incomplete planning pairs retain explicit reasons rather than disappearing.

## Validation and execution

Schema tests enforce keys, grains, accepted reasons and tiers, and nonnegative measures. Singular tests check forecast compatibility and coverage, exact snapshot selection, inbound classification and uniqueness, deterministic supplier selection, monthly-to-daily reconciliation, population preservation, and source/destination transfer caps. Parse and compile are read-only; building the inventory-intelligence relations requires explicit DEV approval:

```powershell
.\.venv\Scripts\dbt.exe parse --project-dir dbt --profiles-dir dbt
.\.venv\Scripts\dbt.exe compile --project-dir dbt --profiles-dir dbt --select tag:inventory_intelligence
.\.venv\Scripts\dbt.exe run --project-dir dbt --profiles-dir dbt --select tag:inventory_intelligence
.\.venv\Scripts\dbt.exe test --project-dir dbt --profiles-dir dbt --select tag:inventory_intelligence
```

The Airflow warehouse DAG reaches inventory intelligence only after warehouse refresh and successful forecasting. Its freshness guard rejects the fixed 2024 source history in 2026. A successful fresh forecast replaces `CURRENT_FORECASTS`; the inventory-intelligence task checks compatibility and skips its dbt commands when the new run does not match the fixed historical cutoff and January–March 2025 horizon. Historical DEV validation therefore uses an explicitly approved manual inventory-intelligence build; the DAG must not be altered to treat stale history as a current operational forecast. After a build, reconcile row counts, missing inputs, risk tiers, shortage and reorder totals, exposure, transfer caps, and forecast coverage in DEV. Rebuilding the selected tables/views can reproduce the calculation when the upstream marts and forecast run are unchanged.

### Persisted DEV historical reconciliation

The corrected DEV build produced 120 planning positions, 10,800 daily planning rows, 360 monthly rows, 120 recommendation rows, and 2 positive transfer allocations. All 120 positions are complete for inventory-risk assessment; 114 have reorder inputs available and 6 lack a selected supplier PO, leaving their reorder quantities unavailable. The risk tiers are 5 `CRITICAL`, 28 `HIGH`, 50 `MEDIUM`, and 37 `LOW`. Expected shortage totals 939 units, while the upper-demand stress path totals approximately 2,884.67 units. There are 78 positive reorder recommendations totaling 2,877 whole units and 5 immediate-mitigation flags. Persisted potential revenue exposure at historical realized unit value totals **167,157.510213** source-currency units.

The PO-derived schedule contains 17 future-due in-transit lines and 783 scheduled units; 2 overdue in-transit lines hold 102 uncertain units and 3 open lines hold 166 uncertain units. The cutoff inventory snapshot reports 885 in-transit units as descriptive context only. Daily and monthly scheduled inbound both reconcile to the 783 PO units. The 360 monthly point and upper-demand totals reconcile to the persisted forecast within numeric tolerance; the point total is 4,180 units. The two transfer allocations total 7 units, with no source-surplus, source-protection, or destination-need violations. The inventory-intelligence dbt suite defines 49 tests for future rebuild validation.

The 2024 synthetic source does not include January–March 2025 realized outcomes, so recommendation effectiveness cannot be measured. Uniform within-month demand, period-end PO state, empirical bounds from short history, unknown transfer timing, and missing supplier mappings further limit interpretation.
